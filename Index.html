Will Know How to Stay

a poem

Will this life ever get interesting?
As I whisper to myself
Then, you came, with little to no mystery
Like an open book, and I thought to myself:
"Pretty." And nothing else.
I never wanted to be with you
I never even think of ever talking to you
I didn't even want you in my life
What more does an open book has to offer?

But one thing changed,
and another followed,
then another,
then another—
like a domino falling,
one by one,
until I could no longer remember
which piece began it all.

You were never an open book.

You were something...
something bizarre,
something I could not place
into any definition I knew.

And somehow,
I wanted to know.

You were not simply pretty.
You were something undefined—
like a limit that refuses to settle,
an indeterminate form
that makes you wonder
whether there is an answer at all.

You were the torrent of a river,
strong enough to shake the stones beneath it,
yet gentle enough to make me stay and watch.

You were the sun's ray
that luminates the ground,
the kind of light
that makes even ordinary things
look worth remembering.

You were something...
something mesmerizing.

As time passed,
I wanted to learn more about you.

I wanted to turn every page,
even the ones you tried to hide.
I wanted to read your book
until there was nothing left unread.

But not every chapter was beautiful.

Some were difficult.
Some were painful.
Some were written in words
I did not know how to understand.

Still,
I kept reading.

I was reading you.

And somewhere between the pages,
I realized—

I wanted you.

I talked to you,
not because I wanted to,
but because something in me
was curious about what came next.

And I was amazed
by the things you had accomplished.

Yet there was still a distance
I could not ignore.

You had reached heights
I had already stood upon.
And perhaps because of that,
perhaps because I thought
I had already seen everything—

gradually,
I lost interest.

I didn't want you anymore.

Until I turned the next page.

And there,
I read what it meant
to be beautiful.

Not beautiful in the way
people look at you,

but beautiful in the way
you treat the world
when no one is watching.

I read what it meant
to be kind.

And I realized
that perhaps beauty
was never something
I could measure by the cover.

So I turned another page.

And what I saw
was something
I would never forget.

The title read:

"Help me write the pages of my life."

I stared at it for a while.

"She wants me
to write these pages?"

So I did.

I began writing beside you,
one page at a time,
thinking that perhaps
this was where our story
was meant to begin.

But the more I wrote,
the more I doubted.

Maybe she wanted
someone else beside her.

Maybe the hand
she wanted holding hers
was never mine.

She never specified
who she wanted
to write it with.

And yet,
while I was writing your book,

you were writing mine.

You were writing mine.

And when I finally read
the page you were writing,
there was a sentence
waiting for me at the bottom:

"I want you to write my story,
my love."

You were asking me.

But what does a request
really change?

I never wanted anyone
to write my story.

And I never wanted
to write someone else's
with my own hand.

I was always
the excess of what could be—
the possibility beyond
what anyone could calculate.

So I wanted to stop.

I didn't want
to write your story anymore.

And I didn't want you
writing mine.

I thought perhaps
some stories are better
left unwritten.

Some lives are meant
to be lived without
another hand deciding
where the next page goes.

Then—

you held my hand.

And with the gentleness
of someone who already knew
what I was afraid to say,

you wrote a single word
on the blank page of my life.

"Stay."

"No,"
I replied.

What more
could staying do?

What could one word
possibly change?

Then you handed me
an empty book.

There was no title.

No first chapter.

Not even a cover page.

It was untouched.
Unwritten.
Untainted by ink.

A book that had never
belonged to anyone.

And inside it,
there was a note.

"My love,
don't write my story.

My love,
let's write our story.

A story where
only the two of us exist.

A story so beautiful
that even the world
would be jealous
of what we are.

A story so beautiful
that even the afterlife
would preserve it
after it is written.

A story so beautiful
that it will never end."

I read it again.

And again.

Afterlife.

And the end.

How strange.

How beautiful.

A contradiction
hidden inside a promise.

Perhaps forever
was never meant to mean
that there would be no ending.

Perhaps forever
was simply this—

that whatever ending comes,
we were there
to write it together.

And suddenly,
the blank pages
didn't frighten me anymore.

They called to me.

For the first time,
I didn't want to know
what the next page contained.

I wanted to write it.

With you.

Not your story.
Not mine.

Ours.

Because maybe
I was never meant
to finish your book.

Maybe you were never meant
to finish mine.

Maybe we were meant
to close both of them,

and begin again
with empty pages.

A story with no certainty.
No predetermined ending.
No promise that every chapter
would be beautiful.

Only two people
holding the same pen,

turning the same pages,

and choosing—
again and again—

to stay.

Pretty,
with all the more she offers
I wanted to be with you
I always thought of sharing everything with you
And I want to build this life with you
Because
Our book has so much to offer
